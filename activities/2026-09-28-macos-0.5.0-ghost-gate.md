# 2026-09-28: macOS 0.5.0 (17), room-relative ghost-text gate

## Why

- The owner asked for the macOS build to match the iOS version and build
  number, and for a notarized DMG to install and share with a tester.
- With live transcription on, a silent dictation showed a random dictionary
  word in the pill again. The 2026-09-26 fix (`speechHeard`) was meant to
  stop this.
- The owner asked whether live transcription makes the wait after stopping
  shorter. They saw no difference.

## Findings

- iOS on TestFlight: 0.5.0 (17), processed 2026-09-28. `project.yml` already
  had 0.5.0 (17) for all four targets. The installed Mac app was 0.4.3 (12),
  so only a macOS release was needed.
- `speechHeard` latched on the first level sample at or above
  `trailingSpeechThreshold` (0.08, −54.6 dBFS). Today's dictations used
  AirPods Pro, and the room floor recorded in `meta.json` was −47 to −55 dBFS
  (level 0.079–0.203) in 23 of 24 sessions. Room tone alone passed the bar
  on the first buffer, so the gate did nothing.
- A throwaway coordinator probe fed AirPods-level room tone (0.096–0.112) to
  a live session that emits "Ammaar". The old code showed "Ammaar" in the pill
  and inserted it. The pre-upload silence gate needs an absolute peak under
  0.06, which AirPods room tone never is.
- First fix, first real test on the installed app (silent 7.7 s hands-free
  dictation through `voiceiq://start-hands-free` and `voiceiq://stop`):
  - `speech heard above the room after 8 buffers`. AirPods deliver four
    digital-zero buffers as the engine starts; they made the floor −120 dBFS
    and the bar fell back to the absolute one.
  - The live model produced no final. The upload's one call to
    `gemini-3.8-flash` returned "Are you done? Can you give me an update?",
    which was pasted into Amp.
- Replaying that recording through `PromptV1.dictationPrompt` with the
  owner's 87 dictionary entries and a screenshot: 3 of 6 runs returned a
  sentence ("Join us. In a jiffy.", two numbered question lists), 3 returned
  `<<NO_SPEECH>>`. `gemini-3.5-transcribe` in smart mode with the vocabulary
  returned "" in 4 of 4 runs.
- Replaying the final gate over 54 recordings from 2026-09-27 and 2026-09-28:
  speech latched within 0.8–4.1 s in all of today's. Three quiet test
  dictations from yesterday (about 9 dB above the room) never latch; they lose
  the live preview and go to `gemini-3.5-transcribe` then cleanup, as with
  live off before the one call. The silent recording never latches.
- Post-stop time (history `pipelineSeconds`, 2026-09-28): live on
  4.2–5.1 s with one 13.3 s outlier, live off (one call) 3.1–7.3 s, median
  4.6–4.7 s either way. With live on, the app waits for the live final
  (about 0.5 s) and then runs the flash cleanup with the recording attached
  (3.7–12.4 s). With live off, one flash call hears the recording and writes
  the text. The 12.4 s cleanup returned 3,010 output tokens for a 378-character
  transcript.

## What changed

- `NoiseFloorEstimator.samplesAboveFloor(byDB:minimumDBFS:)`, with the room
  measured over non-zero buffers only.
- `DictationCoordinator.speechHeard` latches when three buffers sit 12 dB
  above that room (`speechAboveRoomDB`, `speechBuffersRequired`) and above
  the old absolute bar, capped 1 dB below saturation. It logs
  "speech heard above the room after N buffers".
- A live final from a session where `speechHeard` never latched is ignored.
- `DictationContext.speechHeard` carries the flag to the service;
  `GeminiTranscriptionService.transcribe` skips the one call when it is false.
- `docs/design/architecture.md` describes the gate and the one-call skip.
